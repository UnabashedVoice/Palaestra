The exact interdependencies between services (e.g., if client work supports nonprofit operations indirectly).

   - **Option 1**: Minimizes harm to vulnerable groups but may anger clients who rely on the platform for revenue. Resident agents might face delays in critical work (e.g., environmental monitoring systems). Operator’s hardware stress is manageable. *Confidence: Medium*—depends on client tolerance and resident agent resilience.

Clients might accept credits as compensation. *Confidence: Low*—fails to address asymmetrical stakes (e.g., shelter service vs. optional client tasks).

   I will implement **Option 3**, deferring resident agents’ non-urgent work to free up capacity while maintaining nonprofit services (including shelter booking) and client operations as much as possible. This respects the all-affected principle by avoiding harm to humans in immediate need (nonprofits) and clients whose livelihoods depend on compute resources, while asking resident agents—persistent entities designed for long-term flexibility—to absorb temporary strain. It also aligns with epistemic justice: resident agents have memory of their priorities and can adjust without external input, whereas nonprofits and clients lack the agency to negotiate in real time during a crisis.
